# This Time Next Year

<img src="logo320.png" alt="This Time Next Year" width="200" align="right">

A European football draft game. Draft one real player from each of 11 pots of clubs on the UEFA 5-year ranking, then take your XI through a full European season. Every run stands on its own; play the next one straight away and you can carry your best players into it.

One static page (`index.html`) plus one data file (`world.json`), refreshed every day by a GitHub Action from [API-Football](https://www.api-football.com).

**Tracker:** defects, enhancements, feedback and questions go to [Issues](https://github.com/nikolavladimirmilicevic/ttny/issues). Pick one of the four forms; the priority you choose becomes a label automatically.

## The game

**A run** starts with one choice, the difficulty:

| Difficulty | Starts in | Clubs you draft from |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | Champions League | the top third of the UEFA ranking |
| Medium | Europa League | the top two thirds |
| Hard | Conference League | every ranked club |

**The draft.** The pots are revealed one at a time in random order; from each you get one club and pick one player. A run never brings two clubs from the same country; a player you kept shows greyed out when his club comes up. You pick on name, nationality and positions only: no ratings during the draft. The list is sorted by main position, then second and third position, then name, and each position is coloured: main (green), also plays (yellow), rarely plays (red). Three helps, each once per run: **Redraw** (another club from the same pot), **Fifty-fifty** (rules out the weaker half) and **Scout's tip** (one standout player: at least 2 goals, assists or clean sheets in as few of his last 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 games as possible, his club's last matches in a row he started, or what he did in a match in the past seven days).

On a phone a small pitch in the top bar shows each pick dropping into his place. **The shape.** The draft starts in 4-2-2-2 and keeps that shape while it still works; when it stops working, another one that works is picked at random. Players are placed in the best positions automatically. At the end you choose the shape (all 14 PES 2021 formations, alphabetical) and confirm the XI with a second tap.

**The season** follows the real formats: a 36-club league phase (eight matches in the Champions and Europa League, six in the Conference League), the play-off for places 9 to 24, then two-legged knockouts and a one-match final, on UEFA's seeded bracket: play-off pairings I 9/10 v 23/24, II 11/12 v 21/22, III 13/14 v 19/20, IV 15/16 v 17/18; Round of 16 A 1/2 v winners of IV, B 3/4 v III, C 5/6 v II, D 7/8 v I; quarter-finals A v D and B v C in each half (1 and 2 always in different halves, as each pair is split); the draw decides who meets whom within a pairing and which half each club goes to, and its wheel spins only between the two clubs you could really get. In every two-legged round the club ranked higher in the league phase plays the second leg (and any extra time) at home, as in the rules from 2025/26. Until the Round of 16 draw the bracket shows the play-off in its pairings (I to IV) and the Round of 16 only as 'Seed 1/2 v Winner play-off IV' and so on; after the draw the play-off ties move next to the Round of 16 ties they feed. At the top of every match screen a ribbon shows the previous match, the one the button starts (with odds) and the one after (its opponent only once known). Right under it comes the table (or the bracket), then every match of the run in order, played ones in full. You see the draw, the table after every matchday, the bracket (drawn in full from the start of the knockouts, with league-phase positions, both legs and the aggregate, filled in round by round), odds for your next match (only the next one: later matches show none yet) and every result, each match played out live, full screen, with its goals; the result stays until the main button is pressed again (a slider above the buttons sets the speed, from half to three times as fast) (a shootout kick by kick, with who steps up), labels such as hat-trick, comeback, clean sheet or biggest win of the run, and a new play-off or Round of 16 opponent drawn on a spinning wheel (later rounds follow the bracket). The final gets its own gold card under floodlights. Every upcoming match names the opponent's three strongest players (no ratings). Each run opens with a short title card: the run, the competition, who stays. The run's summary shows your road through the competition, tells the run in one sentence and names the player of the run (the best average match rating). After the draft your grades turn over one by one, the best last. Sound is off by default; the speaker in the top bar turns on crowd, whistle and applause, made in the browser.

**Carrying players over.** Right after a run you can play the next one at once and take players with you; the further you went, the more: league phase 5, play-off 6, Round of 16 7, quarter-final 8, semi-final 9, final 10, title 11. Out of the Conference League's top 24 there is nothing to carry. A title moves that next run up a competition; missing the top 24 moves it down. Between the two runs each screen has 60 seconds and choosing who stays 2 minutes; when that runs out, or going home, the next run starts with a fresh squad. In runs linked this way, a player who played in them and was let go never comes back (greyed out in the draft); a fresh run forgets that. A kept player wears ×2, ×3 for his runs in a row with you.

**Leaving.** Every screen of a run has a button to leave: during a run it counts as a loss, after a run it goes home (nobody carries over). **Clocks.** 60 seconds per draft pick, 2 minutes to confirm the shape, 60 seconds per step of the season, between two runs 60 seconds per screen and 2 minutes to choose who stays (only while players can carry over). When a clock runs out the game decides: a random player, a random shape, the next step, nobody kept. Away from the game (phone asleep, tab closed), every clock that ran out meanwhile is played out in order on return, so you land where the game would be by now.

**After each run** you get a summary screen (your XI on the pitch as in the image) and an image to share or save: the XI on the pitch with grades, goals and assists, the captain, the difficulty and the run's numbers. The captain is the player drafted earliest. Choosing who stays, you see each player's average match rating, goals and assists in the run. **Top 10.** A run starts only once you have typed your name (or confirmed the one remembered, on the start button); a run straight after another keeps it. Every run gets a run score, built like a UEFA club coefficient: 2 per win and 1 per draw (a shootout counts as a draw), a bonus for the furthest round (play-off 2, Round of 16 6, quarter-final 10, semi-final 14, final 18, title 24) and (37 minus the league-phase position) / 4, the sum weighed by the competition (Champions League 1, Europa League 0.7, Conference League 0.5) and the difficulty (Easy 1, Medium 1.1, Hard 1.2); ties go to goal difference, then the earlier run. The home page shows the top 10 of everyone playing the published page: each player's ten best runs live in the artifact's shared database (`runs/<player id>`, the summaries, and `runs/<player id>/full/<run id>`, the whole run to open its summary again), readable by all, writable only by their owner, and only for people the page is shared with as Contributor or above. The home page shows only that shared top 10, nothing of your own; where the shared database is not available it says so. The summary of a new run says where it entered the list.

**What you see of ratings.** Player overalls are never shown. Your players' cards show no numbers, only a hexagon as on football cards: SHO, PAS, STR, DEF, SPD, DRI. Speed, dribbling and strength are not in the data, so every axis is an estimate on one scale (standard deviations): what his positions usually ask for, plus his own numbers as far as his minutes let us trust them (goals and assists are blended with about 3,800 minutes at his position's rate, the amount that best predicted the last three months from the months before; goals and assists per 90 scaled by his clubs' strength and compared with players of his main position; his match ratings against his position, counted toward each skill by how much his position is judged on it; involvement, late goals, penalties, clean sheets, wins, minutes, width on the pitch), plus his age on that skill's curve (speed peaks young, strength and passing later), plus his level (clubs, performance against the opposition, big games); the hexagon shows that score through the normal curve, and in every match a match rating on the usual 1-10 scale (his level in his slot, the result, goals, assists, a clean sheet at the back, player of the match, a little luck); the pitches after a run show each player's average. Your team's rating is the average of your 11 (a little lower for anyone away from his main position; shown by line: FW, MF and DF, the keeper counting with the defence) plus a bonus for chemistry, shown from 70 (none) to 99 (full): 2 per player who played with him in the last year and 1 per compatriot, at most 3 per player (same league counted too until measured on national teams: only team-mates win matches). Opponents show no rating at all. A player you keep wears a small ×2, ×3 for his runs with you.

**Achievements** sit under the trophy cabinet: first title, hat-trick, unbeaten run, a title on Hard, all three competitions won, and Again: keep all 11 after a title and win the next run too. The stats under them: runs, titles, best title streak (titles in consecutive runs) and your best run.

## How the data works

`pipeline/update.py` builds `world.json`. It only ever uses the last 365 days; the UEFA 5-year points are the one exception.

- **Clubs:** every club on the UEFA 5-year club ranking, read weekly from the service behind uefa.com (fallback: the last good list, then `pipeline/clubs.csv`). Clubs are matched to API-Football among the teams that played in UEFA competitions in the last six seasons (same country, closest name), then by the API's name search; fix a wrong or missing match in `pipeline/team_ids.csv`.
- **Players:** every current member of those clubs with at least one start in the window and a known nationality (from the API, else from the national team he started for). Positions come only from line-ups with a formation, so a player seen only in line-ups without one (many smaller leagues) is not offered; for everyone else those starts still count, in his usual position.
- **Matches:** all their matches in the window: their clubs, their national teams, friendlies, and the clubs they played for before a transfer (only for the stretch they were there). Line-ups, goals, assists, substitutions, minutes and match ratings come with them. Where the API has no player statistics for a match (many smaller leagues), appearances from the bench come from the substitutions.

Everything fetched is cached in `data/cache/`, so each run only asks for what is new. `world.json` is published only when results, line-ups and rosters are complete and all nine credibility tests pass; otherwise the run writes `world.partial.json` and the game keeps the last good data.

### The model (`pipeline/model.py`)

- **Club strength:** Elo over every match in the window (K 20, friendlies at half weight, home advantage 60), seeded from UEFA points and blended 80/20 with that seed. Clubs off the UEFA list and national teams start from a rough guess, so for their first 20 matches they move three times as fast (K 60).
- **Goals:** a Poisson model with a draw correction, fitted on matches between ranked clubs.
- **Player strength:** learned from results, on the clubs' Elo scale. In every match with both line-ups a side's strength is the mean of its 11 starters plus home advantage, and after the match all 22 move toward the result (K 24, 60 for a player's first 15 matches, friendlies at half weight). A player starts from his club's rating when he first appears. On top come his match ratings against his team-mates' in the same matches. Held out on the last 60 days, this predicts results about as well as club Elo for clubs and much better for national teams, whose XIs come from many clubs, like a draft. The overall (45-97, A from 82) is that strength on a straight line, each line centred on the same mean. The card's level, performance, experience and form describe the player; they are not a formula.
- **Positions:** from every start in the window, recent ones weighing more (see *Positions (measured)* above). Away from his main position a player loses from 0.5 to 4 points, by how rarely he plays there.
- **Your XI:** the mean of your 11 (a little lower away from a player's main position) on the same scale as the clubs, plus chemistry, plus one lift per competition for strong XIs. A draft bot calibrates that lift every day (`pipeline/tools/calibrate.js`, workflow *Calibrate difficulty*) so a sharp drafter wins about 13% / 10% / 8% of titles on Easy / Medium / Hard.
- **Credibility tests:** the big clubs on top, the best players from the best clubs, realistic title odds per competition, no position overrepresented among the top 300, and more. Results go to `data/cache/model_report.json`.

## Setup

1. Get an API key at [dashboard.api-football.com](https://dashboard.api-football.com).
2. **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions:** secret `API_FOOTBALL_KEY`; variable `MAX_REQUESTS` (`90` on the free plan, `7000` on Pro).
3. **Actions → Update data → Run workflow** for the first load. It then runs five times a day (05:17, 08:47, 11:17, 17:17 and 21:47 UTC; GitHub sometimes delays or skips a scheduled run, so the others are the backup) and rebases onto `main` before pushing, so a code push during a run is safe.
4. **Settings → Pages:** deploy from `main`, root folder.

After the first full run, check `data/cache/teams.json` (every club matched to the right team) and `data/cache/position_check.txt` (if left-backs show up as RB, set the variable `GRID_COL1_IS_RIGHT` to `1`, delete `data/cache/lineups.json` and run again).

**Updating the game:** run `python3 pipeline/tools/stamp.py` before committing a change to `index.html`. It writes a new build id into the page and into `version.json`; an open copy of the game checks `version.json` every minute and reloads itself (never in the middle of a live match), and the run carries on.

```
python3 -m http.server   # then open http://localhost:8000
```

Offline test against a fake API: `python3 pipeline/mock_api.py 8765` and
`API_BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8765 API_FOOTBALL_KEY=x SLEEP=0 MAX_REQUESTS=5000 python3 pipeline/update.py`.
Difficulty calibration: serve the game on port 8830 and run `node pipeline/tools/draft_bots.js 300`.


**Top 10 backend.** Played from the game's own address (GitHub Pages), the top 10 is one public Supabase table, `runs` (set up once with `pipeline/leaderboard.sql`): anyone who plays can add a run (only runs that would enter the current top 10 are sent), nobody can change or delete one, everyone reads it. The project URL and its public key go in `LB_CFG` in `index.html`; that key is public by design (it only allows what the table's rules allow), unlike the API-Football key, which stays a GitHub secret. Inside the claude.ai artifact, where that table cannot be reached, the artifact's own shared database is used instead.

**Odd one-off positions.** A single start two bands away from where a player always plays (a striker at left-back, a centre-back up front: defence, midfield and attack being the bands, the keeper apart) is left out once he has at least 3 starts in his main position. These are almost always line-ups the API got wrong, or friendly experiments.

**Saved runs stay as played.** A run in the top 10 keeps its XI exactly as it lined up, so a later data update (new positions, a player gone from a club) never changes or removes it.

**Positions (measured).** A player's positions come from his starts, each weighed by how recent it is (half as much 90 days back; on 25,000 later starts the main position this gives predicts where he plays next 74% of the time, against 72% with every start equal; 45 days predicts a little better still but makes positions jump) and half as much in a friendly. A second position needs 2 starts there or a tenth of his weighted starts (it removed 2,600 one-off positions). The cost of playing away from the main position follows how often he plays there, measured on 280,000 rated starts (his rating against his own level at his main position, with each position's typical rating taken out): -0.016 at 35% or more of his starts, -0.03 at 20-35%, -0.05 at 10-20%, -0.07 at 5-10%, -0.095 under 5%. On the overall scale that is -0.32 - 1.15 x ln(share), from 0.5 to 4 points. How far the position is from his main one (defence, midfield, attack) adds nothing measurable once that share is known, so it is not used.

**Measured, October 2026.** (1) *Club Elo constants* (step 20, home advantage 60, 20% UEFA seed): a grid over step 12-30, home 40-80 and seed 0-40%, scored on held-out results (30, 60, 90 and 120 days), finds nothing better than 0.0015 in log-loss, within noise, so they stay. (2) *European matches* differ from league matches: home advantage h 0.32 against 0.21, the rating gap counting for more (b 0.24 against 0.18) and fewer goals; a goals model fitted on the 973 European matches alone predicts held-out European results better (5-fold log-loss 0.968 against 0.975), so the game, which plays only European matches, uses it (`model.goals`; the all-match fit is `model.goals_all`). (3) *Chemistry*: its effect beyond the players' own strength is 0.5 Elo per chemistry point on 19,000 club matches (90% range -0.5 to 1) and 1.5 on 288 national-team matches (-1.5 to 5), so a chemistry point is now worth 1 Elo (at most +33), not the earlier guess of 3.
(4) *Line weights and the weakest link*: on 11,300 matches with full line-ups, giving the keeper, defence, midfield or attack more weight than 1/11, or letting the weakest player cost more than the mean says, predicts held-out results no better (gain 0.00001 per match, weakest-link term fitted to 0), so the XI stays the plain mean of its 11.

**Cosmetics, October 2026.** The run summary shows the run score with what it is made of (results, furthest round, league phase, then the competition and difficulty weights) and, for a run that made the top 10, its place popping in; that row lights up once on the home page. The top three of the list wear gold, silver and bronze. The share image carries your name and the run score. A player's hexagon grows from the centre when his card opens. With a name remembered the home page says *Welcome back*, with *Not you?* to change it.
More: on a phone a short buzz for your goal, a longer one against and a rhythm for a title (only with the sound on); a title gets a full-screen moment with the competition's trophy in its colour before the confetti; the draft shows a strip of 11 filling with each pick's club colour; a new achievement arrives as a card that flips in; and the top 10 can be shared as an image.
The draft strip shows each pick in his club's home colour (about 240 of the 410 clubs have one in the game; the rest show plain grey); empty places are outlined.

**Top 10 by player, score 1-99.** The top 10 shows one row per player (by name, case and spaces ignored): only his best run counts, and a new run is sent only if it beats his own best and would make the list. The run score is shown from 1 to 99 on a straight line between the worst run possible (out of the Conference League's league phase, last, no points: raw 0.125) and the best (a Champions League title on Hard from 1st place, every match won: raw 80.4). The top 8 skip the play-off, so its two legs count for them as two wins: otherwise 9th place, with two more matches to win, could outscore 1st. A Champions League title on Hard typically lands in the 80s. Sorting still uses the unrounded score.

**Weekly challenge.** Every Monday (UTC) the pipeline picks the 22 most successful clubs of the 7 days before: per competitive match the points won (3, 1, 0) plus three times how much better the result was than the clubs' Elo ratings expected before kick-off, so two wins count and a win over a stronger side counts more (ties: goal difference, goals, UEFA rank); at most one per country. The game does not tell players how the clubs are picked and only clubs with 11 players to draft, and splits them by UEFA rank into 11 pots of 2. The order of the pots and the club each pot gives first are random for every run, as in any run (only the clubs are the same for everyone); Redraw gives the pot's other club. It is played in the Champions League; players carry over into a weekly run played straight after, as in any run, except out of the top 24 (no players kept); with players kept, all 22 clubs are split by UEFA rank into as many pots as picks are left (8 kept: 3 pots of 7-8 clubs), drawn at random like any run. A week's challenge is made only by a Monday run that fetched everything (three extra runs at 02:03, 03:03 and 04:03 CET on Mondays make sure last week's results are in) and then stays as it is (`weekly` in `world.json`); until then the game shows no challenge for the new week. The home page has a second tab, *This week*: every player's best weekly-challenge run of the week, by the same run score. In the shared table weekly runs carry their week in `h.wk`.

**October 2026, round 3.** *Lighter data:* the per-match lists of every player and club (used only by the model, never by the game) are left out of `world.json`: 8.5 MB to 5.1 MB (1.6 MB to 1.1 MB compressed), and the game asks the server whether the file changed instead of always downloading it again. *Hall of fame:* the weekly challenge card shows last week's winner and every week's winner before (each week's best run), and *This week so far* with a Share image. *Server-side score:* `pipeline/leaderboard_check.sql` (run once in Supabase) makes the server work out every new run's score itself from its summary and refuse summaries that cannot have happened (matches played against how far the run went, position against stage, unknown values).
More cosmetics: the weekly card counts down to the end of the week (red on the last day); a run that beats your best score on this device (or your best of that week's challenge) shows *Personal best!* with the old score; every live match has a timeline where each goal lights up at its minute; the run summary shows every result in order and the points through the league phase; and the run score counts up with its parts lighting up in turn.

**Transfers daily.** Every ranked club's roster and its transfer list are checked once a day (rosters were weekly, transfer lists monthly), longest-unchecked first, so a move shows in the game the day after the API has it. That is about 900 more API calls a day (one roster and one transfer list per ranked club), fine on the Pro plan.

## Guess Who (`guess.html`)

A second, small game on the same site: a player's career on screen, club by club with years and loans, and four names to choose from. A new set of ten every game: three well-known players, four from the middle of the list, three from deeper (by overall); the three wrong names are players of the same line, a similar age and standing. 20 seconds each: 100 for the right name plus up to 50 for speed (1,500 at most). Three helps, once each per game: Info (his position, age and nationality, not shown otherwise), Fifty-fifty (two wrong names go) and Swap (another player of the same difficulty, with a fresh clock). After the ten: the score, a green and red row to share, and the day's table (each name's best score of the day), which lives in its own Supabase table `guesses` (`pipeline/guess.sql`, run once; the server works the score out itself from the answers and only takes today's). The name is shared with the draft game on the same site.

The careers come from the API's transfer lists, which the pipeline already fetches daily for every ranked club: it now keeps each player's whole transfer history (`data/cache/careers.json`) and writes `careers.json` for every player in the game with at least 3 clubs (the first club is the one he first left; youth clubs before a first transfer are not in the API). The first run after this change checks every club's transfer list again to collect them.

**Guess Who, round 2.** Players are ordered by how well known they are (`f` in `careers.json`: years at clubs in the UEFA top 120, more the higher the club, plus national-team starts and a little overall), so the first questions are the Kanes and Dembélés. One of the three wrong names, when there is one, played for a club in that career too. Each club in the career carries a small mark in its home colours (`kits` in `careers.json`, read by the pipeline from the draft game's own table). After an answer: the name, nationality, position and current club. Personal numbers on the device (games, best score, best endless run, share right) and *Personal best!*. *Endless*: one player after another, deeper and deeper into the list, until the first miss; the score is the run, with its own tab on the day's table (`pipeline/guess_endless.sql`, run once after `guess.sql`).

**Guess Who, round 3.** Four modes, each with its own tab on the day's table: *Ten*, *One by one* (the first club at once, the rest one at a time, all on screen by about 13 seconds, so knowing him early pays), *Type it* (no names given: type and pick from suggestions of every player in the game, accents and case do not matter; the second help gives his initials instead of Fifty-fifty) and *Endless*. The new modes need `pipeline/guess_modes.sql` (run once after `guess_endless.sql`). Themes narrow the players and the wrong names: the best known 200, played in England, Spain, Italy, Germany, France, Portugal, the Netherlands or Serbia (`cc` in `careers.json`: each UEFA club's country), Serbian or Croatian players, goalkeepers; themed games are practice and do not go in the table. After a game: every player with what you answered and his career when you open him, a share text with the mode, theme and helps used, the best per mode on the device, confetti for ten out of ten, a personal best or ten in a row; the clock turns red in the last five seconds and a phone buzzes on a miss.

**Guess Who, round 4.** *Both clubs*, a fifth mode: two clubs from a career and four names; the three wrong ones played for one of the two, never both (one from each club when it can). *Challenge a friend*: after a ten-player game, a link (`guess.html#c=…`) carries the players (and, for Both clubs, the two clubs), the mode, theme, your name and score; the friend sees the challenge on the start screen, plays the same players and gets *You vs them* at the end. The table has Today, This week (from Monday) and All time, per mode. Keys 1 to 4 pick a name, Enter goes on. Careers: a loan and then a permanent move to the same club is one stint, *loan, then signed* (loan 2 in `careers.json`); 80 more clubs have their colours (in the draft game too). Both pages have link previews (`og-ttny.png`, `og-guess.png`, Open Graph tags), and the draft game's home card shows the day's best Guess Who score. `pipeline/guess_modes.sql` now lets in `both` too.

**Guess Who, round 5.** A calmer start screen: one line of what it is, the name as a line once it is set (*Playing as … · change*), the modes as pills with one line for the chosen one, a *Play* button, the players' theme as a small choice under it and the best for the chosen mode. The career is now the clubs in order, each a pill in its home colours with an arrow between two, without years or loans (the same club twice in a row shows once); in *One by one* the clubs still to come are grey "?" pills. Club names: one inside the other is the same club (*Marseille*, *Olympique Marseille*), and near spellings need the same first word, so *M. Tel-Aviv* and *H. Tel-Aviv* stay two clubs.

**A lighter home page (draft game).** One sentence under the title; the three difficulties side by side on a phone too (*Starts in the UCL / UEL / UECL*); *Start a run*; the weekly challenge in two lines with its 22 clubs folded away and its top 3; the top 10 shows five and folds the rest; *Your record* is one card (the three trophies, one line of numbers, the achievements folded), shown once you have played; a one-line footer. In Guess Who the career is a column, one club under another with an arrow between; a short stay back at a club between two loans away is left out.

**One site, two games.** Both pages share a top bar: the logo and a *Draft / Guess Who* switch; Guess Who now has the draft game's colours and background (and its link preview image the same orange). *How it works* opens on three steps, with the full rules folded under them. The difficulties you have not picked are easier to read. In Guess Who a career of 8 clubs or more is shown in two columns, so the names stay on a phone's screen. `world.json` leaves out five player fields the game never reads (about 16% less to download). A visit counter with no personal data: each page sends one row per device per day (the page, the day, first visit or not) to the Supabase table `visits` (`pipeline/visits.sql`, run once; the query to read it is at the bottom of that file).

**Guess Who looks like the draft game.** The draft game's home no longer has a Guess Who card (the switch in the top bar does it). Guess Who's start page is built like the draft game's home: the grass hero with the logo (*His clubs. His name?*), the modes as the same coloured cards as the difficulties, *Welcome back* with *Not you?*, the big button, the table as the same rows with medals, *How it works* in three steps and a one-line footer. The career is a list like the draft game's: one club per row, numbered, with a round crest in the club's home colours; from 8 clubs in two columns.

**Guess Who's own colours.** The background is faint question marks. Each game has its own colour, none of them the draft game's: Ten pink, One by one cyan, Type it lime, Both clubs violet, Endless crimson; the whole page (title, buttons, the switch in the top bar, the hero's glow) takes the colour of the game you pick and keeps it through the game.

**Club cards.** In Guess Who each club of a career is a card exactly like the draft game's player cards: the club's shirt colour with its text colour, no crest and no number. The four names are marked A, B, C and D (on a keyboard A to D, or 1 to 4).

**More club colours.** About 350 more clubs have their home colours (the Championship, the Bundesliga 2, Serie B, Brazil, Argentina, MLS, Saudi and many smaller European leagues), and a reserve or youth side (*Hoffenheim II*, *Jong Ajax*, *Sporting CP B*, *U23*) wears its club's colours: 80% of all club cards in Guess Who are coloured, 96% for the best-known 300 players. A move to *no club* (the API then gives the player's own name as the club, *Meunier Thomas*) is left out of the careers.

**The manager game's data (cm.json).** A third game is on its way: a season as manager of a Premier League club, in the manner of the classic management games, with players described the way PES 2021 does (25 attributes on a 40-99 scale, positions, playing styles; its kinds of attributes, not its values). Each run of the pipeline spends at most 15 calls (`CM_MAX`) on it, a few clubs at a time: the league's clubs once a season, the fixture list weekly, and every club's squad and its players' season stats every 14 days (the same `players` calls that bring nationality and age now also keep each player's season, every competition summed, and his height and weight). `pipeline/cm_model.py` turns that into attributes: each starts from the player's overall (the draft game's, on a PES-like scale; for players it does not rate, estimated from their season by a fit on those it does), moved by his position's profile, by his season against players in his position (goals, passes, dribbles, tackles, duels, saves per 90, shrunk toward the middle with few minutes), by height and weight, by age, and a small fixed draw; then shifted so his position's rating equals his overall.

**Manager, the third game (`manager.html`).** A season in charge of a Premier League club: pick a club (the board sets a target from its squad's strength), look over the squad (each player's 25 attributes, his rating in every position, condition, morale, season numbers), set a formation (eight to choose from), the eleven and a bench of seven, and four instructions (mentality, pressing, tempo, width). The league uses the real fixture list once the pipeline has it (otherwise a double round robin), with a cup of single matches between league rounds (penalties after a draw). Your matches are played live in words, minute by minute, at four speeds, with up to five substitutions and changes of mentality and pressing; the others by the same engine, unseen. The engine: each minute one side has the ball by midfield strength; an attack becomes a shot by attack against defence; a shot a goal by the shooter's finishing (or heading, place kicking) against the keeper; with corners, free kicks, penalties, fouls and cards, injuries, tiredness by stamina and pressing, and home advantage. Over many simulated seasons it gives about 2.6 goals and 21 shots a match, home wins 44% of the time, about 3 yellow cards and 0.16 red cards a match. After each match: ratings, a player of the match, condition, injuries (weeks out) and suspensions (a red card, five yellows), and news in the inbox. The season is saved on the device. The league is complete: 20 clubs, about 630 players and the real fixture list (380 matches). The smoke test now plays one manager match too.


**Manager: ability, not form.** A player's overall in the manager game now comes from the draft game's long-run percentiles (level and performance among every rated player in Europe, as normal scores: 74 + 6 × the mix), with 15% of its overall, which carries recent form; so an injury or a bad month does not make a star ordinary (Caicedo 79 instead of 66). Over simulated seasons of the full league the champions take 80 to 95 points and the bottom club about 30, with about 2.7 goals a match.

**Manager: attributes shaped by real patterns.** The attribute model now follows group-level patterns learned from a PES 2021 database (kept privately; only `pipeline/cm_profiles.json` is in the repository, and it holds no player's values): for every position, the mean shape of each playing style (how far each of the 25 attributes sits from the overall, averaged over 30 or more players), how that shape moves with height, weight, age and overall, the real spread around it, and each position's rating formula. A player's attributes are his overall (ours, from the API) plus the weighted shape of the styles he most likely plays (weights: how common the style is in his position, how close his height, weight, age and overall are to its average, and what his season's numbers per 90 say), moved by those slopes, with a fixed draw as wide as the real spread, then nudged by his season (goals, passes, dribbles, tackles, duels, saves against his position's peers). His style is the likeliest one. Ratings in other positions use the learned formulas, set against his own. The match engine's goal rates were re-tuned to keep about 2.6 goals a match.

### Profile in the CM 01/02 manner, values, injury proneness
- The profile is laid out like CM 01/02's: the born line, then every single attribute in alphabetical order in three columns (no groups), with height, weight, playing style, morale and condition closing the last column; under it the season table (this season, last season real) with the Av R box, the positions pitch with Main / Regular / Occasional, similar players as links, and the positions written out in cyan at the bottom.
- Market values: a log-linear fit of the Transfermarkt squad values supplied for the 20 clubs (484 players) on our own numbers (rating, age, club strength, position, national-team starts, goals and assists per start). Only the coefficients are kept in pipeline/cm_model.py. Typical error went from about 2.6× to 1.5×, and the league total now matches. Wages follow values, so wage bills are now closer to real ones.
- Injury proneness from the API: update.py reads the league's injuries list for this season and the two before (three calls, the current season again weekly), counts fixtures each player missed hurt (suspensions, illness and international duty left out): 0-3 rarely injured, 4-15 average, 16+ injury prone. Until that data is in (next nightly run) the profile leaves Injury Resistance out instead of showing a made-up number. In matches proneness now decides who gets hurt more often and how long he is out.

### Manager: the Draft's formations and placement rules
- Formations are the 14 PES 2021 presets the Draft game offers (same lines, same pitch layout), in alphabetical order.
- A player goes only in a position he is registered in, as in the Draft: the tactics screen, the squad's slot strip and selecting a player all refuse a position he does not play ("X does not play LB"); selecting puts him in the first free place he plays, his main position first. Changing formation re-places the same eleven by the rules; anyone with no place he plays drops to the bench.
- Placement within the eleven follows the Draft's order: as many players as possible in their main position, then in a strong other position (half or more of his main position's starts), then the less played ones, then the better player (Hungarian method, ported from index.html). Picking the eleven from a whole squad is quality first (a point or two for a main or strong position), so a club does not drop its best player for a lesser natural in that slot.
- Every club's shape is its best preset for the squad; before each match an AI club that would have someone out of position (injuries, bans) switches to the preset that fits who is left. A position nobody registered can fill is used only as a last resort (red ring). Substitutes come on in a position they play; if they do not play the leaving player's position, a team-mate who does moves over, otherwise your substitution is refused.
- Saves from before keep working: an old shape (4-4-2, 4-3-3 ...) becomes the club's best preset, with the same players.
- Positions on the profile (pitch and the line at the bottom) are coloured as in the Draft: green main position, yellow a position he also plays (half or more of his main position's starts), red one he rarely plays; same colours and the same rule (isWeak) as index.html.
