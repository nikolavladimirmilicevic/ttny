# This Time Next Year

<img src="logo320.png" alt="This Time Next Year" width="200" align="right">

A European football draft game. Draft one real player from each of 11 pots of clubs on the UEFA 5-year ranking, then take your XI through a full European season. Every run stands on its own; play the next one straight away and you can carry your best players into it.

One static page (`index.html`) plus one data file (`world.json`), refreshed every day by a GitHub Action from [API-Football](https://www.api-football.com).

**Tracker:** defects, enhancements, feedback and questions go to [Issues](https://github.com/nikolavladimirmilicevic/ttny/issues). Pick one of the four forms; the priority you choose becomes a label automatically.

## The game

**A run** starts with one choice, the difficulty:

| Difficulty | Starts in | Clubs you draft from |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | Champions League | the top third of the UEFA ranking |
| Medium | Europa League | the top two thirds |
| Hard | Conference League | every ranked club |

**The draft.** The pots are revealed one at a time in random order; from each you get one club and pick one player. A run never brings two clubs from the same country; a player you kept shows greyed out when his club comes up. You pick on name, nationality and positions only: no ratings during the draft. The list is sorted by main position, then second and third position, then name, and each position is coloured: main (green), also plays (yellow), rarely plays (red). Three helps, each once per run: **Redraw** (another club from the same pot), **Fifty-fifty** (rules out the weaker half) and **Scout's tip** (one standout player: at least 2 goals, assists or clean sheets in as few of his last 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 games as possible, his club's last matches in a row he started, or what he did in a match in the past seven days).

On a phone a small pitch in the top bar shows each pick dropping into his place. **The shape.** The draft starts in 4-2-2-2 and keeps that shape while it still works; when it stops working, another one that works is picked at random. Players are placed in the best positions automatically. At the end you choose the shape (all 14 PES 2021 formations, alphabetical) and confirm the XI with a second tap.

**The season** follows the real formats: a 36-club league phase (eight matches in the Champions and Europa League, six in the Conference League), the play-off for places 9 to 24, then two-legged knockouts and a one-match final, on UEFA's seeded bracket: play-off pairings I 9/10 v 23/24, II 11/12 v 21/22, III 13/14 v 19/20, IV 15/16 v 17/18; Round of 16 A 1/2 v winners of IV, B 3/4 v III, C 5/6 v II, D 7/8 v I; quarter-finals A v D and B v C in each half (1 and 2 always in different halves, as each pair is split); the draw decides who meets whom within a pairing and which half each club goes to, and its wheel spins only between the two clubs you could really get. In every two-legged round the club ranked higher in the league phase plays the second leg (and any extra time) at home, as in the rules from 2025/26. Until the Round of 16 draw the bracket shows the play-off in its pairings (I to IV) and the Round of 16 only as 'Seed 1/2 v Winner play-off IV' and so on; after the draw the play-off ties move next to the Round of 16 ties they feed. At the top of every match screen a ribbon shows the previous match, the one the button starts (with odds) and the one after (its opponent only once known). Right under it comes the table (or the bracket), then every match of the run in order, played ones in full. You see the draw, the table after every matchday, the bracket (drawn in full from the start of the knockouts, with league-phase positions, both legs and the aggregate, filled in round by round), odds for your next match (only the next one: later matches show none yet) and every result, each match played out live, full screen, with its goals; the result stays until the main button is pressed again (a slider above the buttons sets the speed, from half to three times as fast) (a shootout kick by kick, with who steps up), labels such as hat-trick, comeback, clean sheet or biggest win of the run, and a new play-off or Round of 16 opponent drawn on a spinning wheel (later rounds follow the bracket). The final gets its own gold card under floodlights. Every upcoming match names the opponent's three strongest players (no ratings). Each run opens with a short title card: the run, the competition, who stays. The run's summary shows your road through the competition, tells the run in one sentence and names the player of the run (the best average match rating). After the draft your grades turn over one by one, the best last. Sound is off by default; the speaker in the top bar turns on crowd, whistle and applause, made in the browser.

**Carrying players over.** Right after a run you can play the next one at once and take players with you; the further you went, the more: league phase 5, play-off 6, Round of 16 7, quarter-final 8, semi-final 9, final 10, title 11. Out of the Conference League's top 24 there is nothing to carry. A title moves that next run up a competition; missing the top 24 moves it down. Between the two runs each screen has 60 seconds and choosing who stays 2 minutes; when that runs out, or going home, the next run starts with a fresh squad. In runs linked this way, a player who played in them and was let go never comes back (greyed out in the draft); a fresh run forgets that. A kept player wears ×2, ×3 for his runs in a row with you.

**Leaving.** Every screen of a run has a button to leave: during a run it counts as a loss, after a run it goes home (nobody carries over). **Clocks.** 60 seconds per draft pick, 2 minutes to confirm the shape, 60 seconds per step of the season, between two runs 60 seconds per screen and 2 minutes to choose who stays (only while players can carry over). When a clock runs out the game decides: a random player, a random shape, the next step, nobody kept. Away from the game (phone asleep, tab closed), every clock that ran out meanwhile is played out in order on return, so you land where the game would be by now.

**After each run** you get a summary screen (your XI on the pitch as in the image) and an image to share or save: the XI on the pitch with grades, goals and assists, the captain, the difficulty and the run's numbers. The captain is the player drafted earliest. Choosing who stays, you see each player's average match rating, goals and assists in the run. **Top 10.** A run starts only once you have typed your name (or confirmed the one remembered, on the start button); a run straight after another keeps it. Every run gets a run score, built like a UEFA club coefficient: 2 per win and 1 per draw (a shootout counts as a draw), a bonus for the furthest round (play-off 2, Round of 16 6, quarter-final 10, semi-final 14, final 18, title 24) and (37 minus the league-phase position) / 4, the sum weighed by the competition (Champions League 1, Europa League 0.7, Conference League 0.5) and the difficulty (Easy 1, Medium 1.1, Hard 1.2); ties go to goal difference, then the earlier run. The home page shows the top 10 of everyone playing the published page: each player's ten best runs live in the artifact's shared database (`runs/<player id>`, the summaries, and `runs/<player id>/full/<run id>`, the whole run to open its summary again), readable by all, writable only by their owner, and only for people the page is shared with as Contributor or above. The home page shows only that shared top 10, nothing of your own; where the shared database is not available it says so. The summary of a new run says where it entered the list.

**What you see of ratings.** Player overalls are never shown. Your players' cards show no numbers, only a hexagon as on football cards: SHO, PAS, STR, DEF, SPD, DRI. Speed, dribbling and strength are not in the data, so every axis is an estimate on one scale (standard deviations): what his positions usually ask for, plus his own numbers as far as his minutes let us trust them (goals and assists are blended with about 3,800 minutes at his position's rate, the amount that best predicted the last three months from the months before; goals and assists per 90 scaled by his clubs' strength and compared with players of his main position; his match ratings against his position, counted toward each skill by how much his position is judged on it; involvement, late goals, penalties, clean sheets, wins, minutes, width on the pitch), plus his age on that skill's curve (speed peaks young, strength and passing later), plus his level (clubs, performance against the opposition, big games); the hexagon shows that score through the normal curve, and in every match a match rating on the usual 1-10 scale (his level in his slot, the result, goals, assists, a clean sheet at the back, player of the match, a little luck); the pitches after a run show each player's average. Your team's rating is the average of your 11 (a little lower for anyone away from his main position; shown by line: FW, MF and DF, the keeper counting with the defence) plus a bonus for chemistry, shown from 70 (none) to 99 (full): 2 per player who played with him in the last year and 1 per compatriot, at most 3 per player (same league counted too until measured on national teams: only team-mates win matches). Opponents show no rating at all. A player you keep wears a small ×2, ×3 for his runs with you.

**Achievements** sit under the trophy cabinet: first title, hat-trick, unbeaten run, a title on Hard, all three competitions won, and Again: keep all 11 after a title and win the next run too. The stats under them: runs, titles, best title streak (titles in consecutive runs) and your best run.

## How the data works

`pipeline/update.py` builds `world.json`. It only ever uses the last 365 days; the UEFA 5-year points are the one exception.

- **Clubs:** every club on the UEFA 5-year club ranking, read weekly from the service behind uefa.com (fallback: the last good list, then `pipeline/clubs.csv`). Clubs are matched to API-Football among the teams that played in UEFA competitions in the last six seasons (same country, closest name), then by the API's name search; fix a wrong or missing match in `pipeline/team_ids.csv`.
- **Players:** every current member of those clubs with at least one start in the window and a known nationality (from the API, else from the national team he started for). Positions come only from line-ups with a formation, so a player seen only in line-ups without one (many smaller leagues) is not offered; for everyone else those starts still count, in his usual position.
- **Matches:** all their matches in the window: their clubs, their national teams, friendlies, and the clubs they played for before a transfer (only for the stretch they were there). Line-ups, goals, assists, substitutions, minutes and match ratings come with them. Where the API has no player statistics for a match (many smaller leagues), appearances from the bench come from the substitutions.

Everything fetched is cached in `data/cache/`, so each run only asks for what is new. `world.json` is published only when results, line-ups and rosters are complete and all nine credibility tests pass; otherwise the run writes `world.partial.json` and the game keeps the last good data.

### The model (`pipeline/model.py`)

- **Club strength:** Elo over every match in the window (K 20, friendlies at half weight, home advantage 60), seeded from UEFA points and blended 80/20 with that seed. Clubs off the UEFA list and national teams start from a rough guess, so for their first 20 matches they move three times as fast (K 60).
- **Goals:** a Poisson model with a draw correction, fitted on matches between ranked clubs.
- **Player strength:** learned from results, on the clubs' Elo scale. In every match with both line-ups a side's strength is the mean of its 11 starters plus home advantage, and after the match all 22 move toward the result (K 24, 60 for a player's first 15 matches, friendlies at half weight). A player starts from his club's rating when he first appears. On top come his match ratings against his team-mates' in the same matches. Held out on the last 60 days, this predicts results about as well as club Elo for clubs and much better for national teams, whose XIs come from many clubs, like a draft. The overall (45-97, A from 82) is that strength on a straight line, each line centred on the same mean. The card's level, performance, experience and form describe the player; they are not a formula.
- **Positions:** from every start in the window, recent ones weighing more (see *Positions (measured)* above). Away from his main position a player loses from 0.5 to 4 points, by how rarely he plays there.
- **Your XI:** the mean of your 11 (a little lower away from a player's main position) on the same scale as the clubs, plus chemistry, plus one lift per competition for strong XIs. A draft bot calibrates that lift every day (`pipeline/tools/calibrate.js`, workflow *Calibrate difficulty*) so a sharp drafter wins about 13% / 10% / 8% of titles on Easy / Medium / Hard.
- **Credibility tests:** the big clubs on top, the best players from the best clubs, realistic title odds per competition, no position overrepresented among the top 300, and more. Results go to `data/cache/model_report.json`.

## Setup

1. Get an API key at [dashboard.api-football.com](https://dashboard.api-football.com).
2. **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions:** secret `API_FOOTBALL_KEY`; variable `MAX_REQUESTS` (`90` on the free plan, `7000` on Pro).
3. **Actions → Update data → Run workflow** for the first load. It then runs five times a day (05:17, 08:47, 11:17, 17:17 and 21:47 UTC; GitHub sometimes delays or skips a scheduled run, so the others are the backup) and rebases onto `main` before pushing, so a code push during a run is safe.
4. **Settings → Pages:** deploy from `main`, root folder.

After the first full run, check `data/cache/teams.json` (every club matched to the right team) and `data/cache/position_check.txt` (if left-backs show up as RB, set the variable `GRID_COL1_IS_RIGHT` to `1`, delete `data/cache/lineups.json` and run again).

**Updating the game:** run `python3 pipeline/tools/stamp.py` before committing a change to `index.html`. It writes a new build id into the page and into `version.json`; an open copy of the game checks `version.json` every minute and reloads itself (never in the middle of a live match), and the run carries on.

## Run locally

```
API_FOOTBALL_KEY=... MAX_REQUESTS=90 python3 pipeline/update.py
python3 -m http.server   # then open http://localhost:8000
```

Offline test against a fake API: `python3 pipeline/mock_api.py 8765` and
`API_BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8765 API_FOOTBALL_KEY=x SLEEP=0 MAX_REQUESTS=5000 python3 pipeline/update.py`.
Difficulty calibration: serve the game on port 8830 and run `node pipeline/tools/draft_bots.js 300`.


**Top 10 backend.** Played from the game's own address (GitHub Pages), the top 10 is one public Supabase table, `runs` (set up once with `pipeline/leaderboard.sql`): anyone who plays can add a run (only runs that would enter the current top 10 are sent), nobody can change or delete one, everyone reads it. The project URL and its public key go in `LB_CFG` in `index.html`; that key is public by design (it only allows what the table's rules allow), unlike the API-Football key, which stays a GitHub secret. Inside the claude.ai artifact, where that table cannot be reached, the artifact's own shared database is used instead.

**Odd one-off positions.** A single start two bands away from where a player always plays (a striker at left-back, a centre-back up front: defence, midfield and attack being the bands, the keeper apart) is left out once he has at least 3 starts in his main position. These are almost always line-ups the API got wrong, or friendly experiments.

**Saved runs stay as played.** A run in the top 10 keeps its XI exactly as it lined up, so a later data update (new positions, a player gone from a club) never changes or removes it.

**Positions (measured).** A player's positions come from his starts, each weighed by how recent it is (half as much 90 days back; on 25,000 later starts the main position this gives predicts where he plays next 74% of the time, against 72% with every start equal; 45 days predicts a little better still but makes positions jump) and half as much in a friendly. A second position needs 2 starts there or a tenth of his weighted starts (it removed 2,600 one-off positions). The cost of playing away from the main position follows how often he plays there, measured on 280,000 rated starts (his rating against his own level at his main position, with each position's typical rating taken out): -0.016 at 35% or more of his starts, -0.03 at 20-35%, -0.05 at 10-20%, -0.07 at 5-10%, -0.095 under 5%. On the overall scale that is -0.32 - 1.15 x ln(share), from 0.5 to 4 points. How far the position is from his main one (defence, midfield, attack) adds nothing measurable once that share is known, so it is not used.
